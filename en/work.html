Where it started

By mid-2026 the company was paying for 54 different tools, often two or three doing the same job: CRM, email marketing, automations, landing pages, calendars, client management. Software spend had reached 6.3% of revenue, more than twice the target.

Cost wasn't the only problem. Every tool kept its own data, and keeping them aligned took Zapier, Make and a lot of manual work: a lead came into one system, got copied into another, the email went out from a third. Nobody had the full picture, and every calendar or automation hiccup landed on one person's desk.

What we did

We mapped every flow one by one: where each lead comes from, which emails it receives and when, what happens at every stage change. That map became the specification for rebuilding everything inside one platform.

Then the systems were replaced one at a time, without stopping sales: first the CRM, then email, then the integrations. Each step ran alongside the old tool until the new one worked on its own.

What changes for the team

The sales team works on a CRM built around their process: automatic lead assignment, reminders, leaderboard and commissions calculated on cash collected. Each lead's profile also shows which emails they opened and which videos they watched.

Leadership sees revenue, trajectory to target and team performance in real time. Segmenting a list or creating a new automation, which used to take hours across tools, now takes minutes.
